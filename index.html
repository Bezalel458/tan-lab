Always inferring from the outside. Yours is the lab that measures the channel I kept inferring around.

C

my reading of the sugar result · an engineer's version

I read it as two sensors on one stimulus, running at different rates. The tongue is fast and can be spoofed. The gut is slow, and transduces the molecule itself at SGLT-1. Preference is the brain deciding which sensor to trust — and silencing the relay between them stops the decision being made at all. τgut ≈ tens of hours

near-exclusive on sugar by 48 h.
D

what the reading buys

A sweetener is an adversarial input to the fast sensor — it passes the tongue, arrives in the gut, and goes unread there. That's why sweet taste alone can't hold a preference.

MDG is the part that makes the reading work. A non-metabolizable analogue still drives the switch, so the slow sensor is keyed to molecular identity, not to energy. It's a chemical detector, not a calorie meter.

And 24–48 h stops looking like a learning rate and starts looking like an integration time — the window the slow channel needs before its estimate outweighs the fast one.

Which would make individual differences a difference in sensor gain.
